## Subject

## Body

Dear Dr. Citkin,

I recently formalized, in Lean 4 / Mathlib, the finite one-generated Heyting algebras Z_n from your paper "An Algebraic Proof of the Nishimura Theorem" (*Logics* 2024). Z_5 through Z_8 are constructed explicitly and certified one-generated by exhibiting every element as a value of your term sequence x_n in the generator, with your Theorem 6 cited for the identification with Z_n. Two results came out of this that I have not been able to locate — though this is precisely the literature I am not equipped to search well, which is why I am writing — and you are the person best placed to say in five minutes whether they are known.

Both are about *ordinary* elements in your sense (§2.1: neither regular nor dense). Machine-checked:

1. **Uniqueness of the ordinary element.** In any Heyting algebra in which every element is a value of some x_n in g — so in every Z_n, and in F_HA(1) itself, by your Theorem 3 — if g is ordinary, then g is the *unique* ordinary element of the algebra. (The two threshold facts are yours: your §2.1 observes that Z_2–Z_5 contain no ordinary elements, and your Proposition 4(c) gives |A| > 5 for ordinary generators. My question concerns only the uniqueness, which I do not see in the paper. Verified unconditionally by exhaustion at n = 6, 7, 8.)

2. **Z_6 in nature.** The divisor lattice of 12 (≅ the subgroup lattice of ℤ/12ℤ) is one-generated — by the divisor 2 — and hence *is* Z_6 by your Theorem 6. More generally, among divisor lattices of ℤ/nℤ: for n with at most two prime divisors (machine-checked for all exponents), an ordinary element exists iff p² | n with a second prime present, and it is unique iff n = p²q; the least such n is 12. For three or more primes I have machine-checked instances only (e.g. 60 = 2²·3·5 has three ordinary elements), not the general statement — the coordinatewise computation appears to settle it, but I have not formalized that, so please read the characterization as proved for ≤ 2 primes and conjectured beyond.

My two questions: (a) is the uniqueness statement in (1) known or folklore? (b) is the identification in (2) — or the n = p²q characterization — noted anywhere, or does it have a standard explanation?

(If the motivation is of any interest: the ordinary elements are exactly the elements at which a certain four-cell decomposition of the algebra relative to a fixed element is non-degenerate; details gladly on request, but the questions above stand alone.)

## Outcome (sent 2026-06-10; reply received 2026-06-11)

Citkin replied within a day. The adjudication, exactly:

* **Question (a) — uniqueness: folklore, not literature.** His words: "I am not aware of any known proofs that a one-generated algebra contains at most one element that is neither regular nor dense" — and the reason: specialists start from the free one-generated algebra (the Rieger–Nishimura ladder), which "contains a single ordinary element," obtain the finite one-generated algebras as its quotients, and regularity and density are preserved under quotients, so "the conclusion seems to be obvious. Therefore, the answer to (a) is yes."
  **Standing of `nishimura_ordinary_unique` after adjudication:** the *mathematics* is specialist folklore — known in principle, never written down so far as the person best placed to know is aware; the *explicit, machine-checked proof* is apparently the first written one. Neither "new theorem" nor "already published": unwritten folklore, now written. His quotient argument is an independent proof route — note it *presupposes* the single-ordinary-element property of the ladder itself, which is exactly what the term-ladder proof (`four_le_nishimuraTerm` + regularity of `x₁, x₃`) establishes rigorously; the two arguments are consistent, and his is consistent with the `H8` counterexample (it lives strictly inside one-generated algebras, where `H8` does not).
* **Question (b) — `Div12 ≅ Z_6` / `p²q`: not adjudicated.** He read (b) as the one-generated-Heyting-algebra/one-generated-group analogy and answered that question instead. He did not say the divisor-lattice identification or the `p²q` characterization is known. Standing: **unadjudicated; no prior art surfaced**; one short clarification is warranted (draft below), after which it either resolves or stays honestly open.
* **Bonus fact from the reply** (recorded as [C], pers. comm.): a one-generated Heyting algebra of cardinality > 5 always has a **unique generator** — unlike one-generated groups, where generators multiply. Convergent with our results: generators are ordinary (his Prop. 4(c)) and the ordinary element is unique, hence the generator is.
