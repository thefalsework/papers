## Body

Dear Professor Amiot,

I work on the algebra of pitch-class structures in ℤ/12, and a Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization I recently finished turned up an identification I have tried and failed to place. The prior art I *do* know is close enough that the honest question is precisely which part, if any, is new — and you are the person who would know.

What I am **not** claiming, because it is known: the subgroup lattice of ℤ/12 (the divisors 1,2,3,4,6,12) consists of familiar objects — tritone, augmented triad, diminished seventh, whole-tone scale; and a six-element Heyting algebra already lives in this area as the subobject classifier Ω of Noll's topos of triads (Set^𝒯).

What I have **not** been able to place: that same subgroup lattice, with its Heyting structure, is the **free** Heyting algebra on one generator — the Rieger–Nishimura algebra truncated to six elements, Z₆ — generated by the **tritone**, which is its *unique* generator and its unique *ordinary* element (an *a* with *a*, ¬*a* ≠ 0 and ¬¬*a* ≠ *a*). Crucially this is **not** Noll's Ω: checking the orders, Noll's six-element algebra has a single atom, whereas the free one has two (the tritone and its pseudocomplement). So *two different* six-element Heyting algebras arise from ℤ/12 music, and the pitch-class lattice is the free / Rieger–Nishimura one. (All machine-checked.)

A number-theoretic companion: among subgroup lattices of ℤ/n, a unique ordinary element exists iff *n* = *p*²*q* — least instance *n* = 12 (24 and 36 have two; 60 has three). Machine-checked for *n* with at most two prime divisors; instance-checked beyond, so please read it as proved for ≤ 2 primes and conjectured further.
