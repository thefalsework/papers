## Body

Dear Dr. Popoff,

I'll lead with the overlap, since your most recent work is the closest thing I have found to mine. In "On Perfectly Balanced Sets" you and your coauthors catalogue subsets of ℤ_{pqr} computationally and find the catalogue's structure governed by the prime factorisation. I have a machine-checked (Lean 4 / Mathlib) catalogue of a *different* invariant on the same kind of object — the **ordinary**, or kernel-bearing, structure of the subgroup lattice of ℤ/n — and it too is governed entirely by the factorisation, in a way that singles out 12.

The result: put the Heyting structure on the subgroup lattice of ℤ/n (its divisor lattice), and ask when it contains an *ordinary* element (Citkin's term: an *a* with *a*, ¬*a* ≠ 0 and ¬¬*a* ≠ *a* — neither regular nor dense). The answer is purely factorisation-theoretic: prime powers *p^k* have none; *p²q* has exactly one; extra primes multiply them (*p²qr* = 60 has three). The least *n* with a *unique* ordinary element is **12**, and there that element is the **tritone**. (Proved for *n* with ≤ 2 prime divisors; instance-checked beyond.)

To be precise about the overlap with your paper: your "perfectly balanced" and my "ordinary / kernel-bearing" are *different* invariants — yours DFT-flavoured, mine Heyting — on the same ground, ℤ/n indexed by factorisation. I am not claiming they coincide; I am noting that we are cataloguing the same kind of object by the same key.

Two things I cannot place, and you are well positioned to say whether they are known:

1. The subgroup lattice of ℤ/12, with its Heyting structure, is `Z₆` — the unique six-element one-generated Heyting algebra, i.e. the first non-degenerate truncation of the free one-generated (Rieger–Nishimura) algebra — generated by the tritone. A six-element Heyting algebra already lives in this area — Noll's topos-of-triads classifier Ω — but it is a *different* one: checking the orders, Ω has a single atom, whereas `Z₆` has two (the tritone and its pseudocomplement). So two distinct six-element Heyting algebras arise from ℤ/12, and the pitch-class lattice is the Rieger–Nishimura one. Is that noted anywhere?

2. Is the *n* = *p*²*q* characterisation (why 12 is least) known?
