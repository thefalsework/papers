## The remainder of the discrete

### A note on incompleteness below incomputability

Is a philosophy of distinction possible that does not smuggle continuity back in to save it? The question is forced by a standoff Fazi (2019) has already named. On one side, determination is treated as a fall from a prior flow: the discrete is a loss, and whatever a mark cannot finish is referred to sensation, to the virtual, to a process that was already there. On the other, determination is treated as finished the moment it is drawn: in or out, and the boundary is nothing. Between these, the discrete is either rescued by the analogue or flattened into the Boolean. A third position is available, at a grain Fazi did not take as her object. A mark is a discrete act, and it is not a fall. What it cannot finish belongs to the mark, not to a richer outside.

Fazi (2019) argues that computation is not made aesthetic by being given a virtuality it does not have; the relevant indeterminacy is Turing’s incomputability, immanent to the axiomatic procedure (Fazi 2016, 2018). That refusal of virtualization is taken as given here. What follows is not a commentary on incomputability. It asks whether immanent incompleteness has another grain.

That grain is the single determination: a distinction, a mark. To determine is to produce an interior and an exterior. Classical logic treats this production as already finished. If a point is not outside, it is inside; double negation does nothing. Intuitionistic logic, the logic of open regions, does not. An open set need not coincide with the interior of its closure. Points may be restored that the region never occupied. Those points are not abroad. They are a hinterland the mark claims as interior and does not administer. Call this the **remainder of the marked**.

The remainder is not incomputability. Incomputability is the failure of a procedure to totalize itself. The remainder is the failure of a mark to totalize its own inside. They are kin in one respect only, and it is the respect that matters for Fazi’s argument: neither is imported from a continuous outside. Neither is a glitch. Both belong to a discrete operation as its own unfinishedness.

This can be seen without the real line. Let P be the three-point poset {p < q} ⊔ {r}, and let the world be the down-set algebra of P — six open sets, the Alexandrov topology, no continuum. Take the mark to be {p}. Then ¬{p} = {r} and ¬¬{p} = {p, q}. The point q lies in the closure of the mark and not in the mark: hinterland. The point r lies in the negation: abroad. The mark is live. This is the smallest such world; no five-element Heyting algebra has an ordinary element. p is the mark, q the hinterland, r abroad. Nothing here is a real number.

There is a second fact, and it is stricter. The exterior of a mark admits no such remainder. In any Heyting algebra, triple negation collapses: ¬¬¬a = ¬a. What would have been a hinterland of the outside is empty. The unmarked side is finished. Only the marked side can fail to be closed. The asymmetry of the mark is not a diagrammatic taste. It is what negation does when interiors are allowed to be incomplete. A philosophy that treats inside and outside as mirrors has already left the discrete for a symmetry the discrete does not have.

A distinction, then, may be live or not. It is live when there is both an exterior and a remainder — abroad and hinterland together. If there is no hinterland, the distinction already coincides with its closure; it is classical, and the positions relative to it collapse. If there is no abroad, the distinction has swallowed the space; there is nothing it refuses. Liveness is the case in which determination has produced a world with four positions, not two: what is clearly inside; what is clearly outside; what occupies the hinterland; what sits on a real edge, meeting both the mark and its negation. These are not metaphors for computational affect. They are the internal articulation of one discrete act.

Live distinctions have a minimal schema: this three-point poset, this six-element algebra. Any observer for whom a given distinction is live is an observer who still sees a copy of that schema, with that distinction sitting where p sits, and who has not merged the inside with what it excludes. This is a theorem about finite Heyting algebras. It is not a claim about Turing machines. It says: seeing a distinction as live is seeing the smallest live world. The schema is to the mark what a finite-state procedure is to computation: the form the discrete operation takes when it is fully itself.

Observation, in this account, is not a gaze upon a distinction that was already there. It is a policy of identification — a decision as to what is to count as the same, and therefore as to what is to count as ground. In the algebra this policy is a nucleus: inflationary, idempotent, meet-preserving; the observer’s world is the set of its fixed points. Some distinctions survive a coarsening of the policy. Some exist only under it, and would vanish for a finer observer. A dense element, which has no exterior at full resolution, can acquire one when the observer raises the bottom of the world; against that new bottom a hinterland opens that was not there. The distinction is then an effect of forgetting. Refinement that keeps the observer’s bottom never closes a live distinction; only refinement that lowers the bottom — revealing a point beneath what had been taken as ground — can. The observer is not an epistemological extra. It is the regime under which a remainder is or is not apparent.

Incomputability does not come and go with coarse-graining, so Fazi’s account does not need a theory of observation. The remainder does. If the discrete is to be thought as generative at the grain of the mark, generation is relative to a regime. That is not virtualization. The observer is still a discrete operation: a further formal abstraction, not a coupling to lived sensation.

The same structure also appears in the continuum, and it is as well to say so after the discrete case, not before. A threshold unit — a perceptron, a step, a single hyperplane — is a mark that finishes. Under the strict-inequality convention its yes-region is an open half-space: closure adds only the boundary, interior takes the boundary away, the region coincides with its own double negation. Iterated determination can, but need not, leave a seam. Two open intervals of the same class whose closures share a single point produce a remainder at that point: closure restores it, interior cannot take it back. The composed region treats as interior a locus it does not occupy. Exclusive-or, which a single hyperplane cannot compute, is not this phenomenon. Its region, the opposite open quadrants, still coincides with its closure. Linear inseparability and the failure of a mark to coincide with itself are distinct failures. They happen to appear first at the same depth, and they are not the same. The history of the perceptron has been told as a history of what one cut cannot compute. It is also, and distinctly, a history of when composition first fails to be classical. None of this is offered as the ground of the remainder. It is the same schema, read in Opens ℝ.

This note does not recast incomputability as a hinterland, or a hinterland as an incomputable function. Fazi’s leftover and this leftover sit at different grains of the same refusal: not to save the discrete by making it continuous. It does not settle whether the mark requires a continuous prior. Fazi’s argument does not require one. A residual commitment in some of the author’s other writing has said otherwise. The mathematics of the remainder does not use that prior, and the view is not founded upon it here. It does not say that computation is life, or that trained networks exhibit a remainder of mass. The algebra says composition *can* leave a seam. The geometry of piecewise-linear decision regions, in at least one measured class, mostly refuses. That refusal is a fact about a geometry, not a refutation of the remainder.

The question, then, is only this. Fazi found immanent indeterminacy in the discrete procedure, and found it without virtualizing the digital. Is it intelligible, in that same refusal, that a single discrete distinction should have an interior the distinction itself cannot finish — and that this unfinishedness should have a shape, an observer, and a smallest world in which it is fully itself? If that is only a local logic of open sets, it can remain so. If it is another incompleteness of the discrete, it belongs beside incomputability, not beneath it, and not as its translation.

### Note on the mathematics

Heyting algebra; Citkin: regular (`¬¬a = a`), dense (`¬a = ⊥`), ordinary = neither. Four-position partition non-degenerate iff ordinary. `¬¬¬a = ¬a`. On Opens X: `¬U = int(Uᶜ)`, `¬¬U = int(cl U)`.

**Discrete schema.** P = {p < q} ⊔ {r}; Down(P) has six elements. For k = {p}: ¬k = {r}, ¬¬k = {p, q}; k ordinary. No Heyting algebra with fewer than six elements has an ordinary element (`Z5.no_kernel`; `ordinary_kernel_div12_embedding`).

**Witness triples.** An observer j_S opens k iff S contains an unspoiled copy of {p, q, r} (`opens_subNucleus_iff_witnessTriple`). A spoiler is a point of k below the abroad-point, or an unanchored outside point below the hinterland-point: the observer has merged the inside with what it excludes.

**Fiber monotonicity [K].** If S ⊆ T and j_S(⊥) = j_T(⊥), then j_S opens k implies j_T opens k (`opens_subNucleus_of_subset_of_bot_eq`). Refinement that keeps the bottom — resolving more finely without lowering the ground — never closes a live distinction. Only adding a point beneath a current minimal point of S can.

**Continuum instances.** A threshold region {x : w·x > θ} (strict inequality) is regular. The composed region (0,1) ∪ (1,2) is ordinary, remainder {1}. The XOR region {xy < 0} is regular.
