## 1. [General] Where does the four-stance lens *fail* in your field?

> The project claims that fields built on one self-undermining founding move tend to produce four stances toward the gap that move leaves open: **work inside it, spread it around, exploit it, or refuse it** (in music: Bach, equal temperament, Coltrane, Schoenberg).
>
> I'm most interested in where this **breaks**. If you work in — or just know well — film, architecture, painting, software, physics, poetry, anything: where does this lens distort more than it reveals? What does it miss, flatten, or force? Counterexamples are more useful to me than confirmations.
>
> No background needed; plain language is perfect.

---

## 2. [General] Is "Refusal" really different from "Exploitation"?

> Two of the four stances are easy to confuse. **Exploitation** keeps the founding move but pushes it past where it wants to resolve, using the not-resolving as material. **Refusal** throws the founding move out entirely and builds on a different principle.
>
> The project argues these are genuinely distinct positions, not two words for "avant-garde." Do you buy that? Is there a work you'd call clearly one and not the other — or one that seems to be both at once, which would be a problem for the claim? Music, film, or anything else welcome.

## 3. [General] "Isn't this just numerology?" — go ahead, make the case

> It's the first objection a lot of people have, so let's have it out in the open.
>
> My short answer: the *music mathematics* is ordinary, machine-checked math (no squinting), while the *cross-field pattern* is an interpretive lens that's stated so it can be wrong — which is the opposite of how numerology behaves. But that's my framing, and I'd rather hear the objection sharpened than pre-empted.
>
> Where does this cross the line from "structure" into "pattern-matching on numbers"? Be specific if you can — it helps me see what to tighten or drop.

## 4. [General] What would convince you the cross-field pattern is real — or fake?

> The music part is proven. The claim that the same four stances run across many fields is *not* — it's the open question.
>
> So: what evidence would move you? What would a real demonstration look like to you, and what would a debunking look like? I have my own answers (a clear field where the stances plainly don't fit; the "exploit" and "refuse" stances collapsing into one), but I want yours — partly because "what would change your mind" is a better conversation than "are you right."

## 5. [General] Musicians: does "the comma is what music is *about*" match your experience?

> Background in two sentences: stacking perfect fifths never closes the circle (the Pythagorean comma), so tuning has a permanent unfixable gap, and equal temperament copes by smearing it across all twelve notes. The project reads a lot of musical practice as different *responses* to that gap.
>
> If you play, compose, tune, or teach: does that framing land, or does it overstate a footnote? Does thinking of a piece by *how it treats the gap* match anything in how you actually hear or make music — or is it a theorist's story that performers never feel?
