# The shape of an aperture in the nucleus lattice

**Companion note to** *The Aperture of a Distinction* (`paper.md`, same directory).
**Date.** 2026-10-28. **Status.** Repository note; results graded per the paper's discipline.
**Artifacts.** `wolfram/aperture-shape.mjs` (sweep; expectations pre-registered in its header), `lean/FalseWorkPapers/Lattice/ApertureCriterion.lean` (the criterion, the fiber theorem, the Div180 witness; axiom audit: `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`, no `sorry`).

## 1. The question

The aperture Ap(k) is a set of nuclei. Nuclei on a Heyting algebra form a lattice (the lattice of subtoposes, or of sublocales), so one can ask what *kind* of subset Ap(k) is. The question was put to us in this form: since ordinariness is "not dense and not regular", is Ap(k) always the intersection of two order-convex families in the nucleus lattice, the observers under which k is not world-dense and those under which it is not world-regular, and is Ap(k) itself always order-convex? The prior evidence pointed that way: on the Rieger–Nishimura truncations Z₆–Z₁₄ the generator's aperture is a principal up-set (every observer above a three-point core), and principal up-sets are convex.

On the down-set algebra D(P) of a finite poset P, nuclei are the sub-ecosystem observers j_S for S ⊆ P (`subNucleusEquiv`, **[K]**), ordered by inclusion of S (S ⊆ T means j_T is finer: j_T ≤ j_S pointwise). The question is therefore finitely checkable on every finite Heyting algebra, since every one is some D(P).

## 2. Answer: convexity fails, and a pointwise criterion holds instead

**Sweep [computed].** All posets on 1–7 points up to isomorphism (1, 2, 5, 16, 63, 318, 2045; the sequence A000112), hence all finite Heyting algebras whose join-irreducibles number at most seven; every nucleus on each (2^|P|, generated by subsets and re-checked against the three nucleus laws; on every algebra with at most 12 elements the count was also cross-checked against brute force over meet-closed families containing ⊤); every kernel k ∈ D(P). 54,723 (algebra, kernel) pairs. The paper's anchors are reproduced exactly (Div12: Ap(2) = {identity}, coAp = 18; Div36: Ap(6) is the two named nuclei; Div24: 3, 3), as is the Z₆–Z₁₄ table (apertures 1, 2, 2, 4, 4, 8, 8, 16, 16; co-apertures 18, 38, 42, 86, 90, 182, 186, 374, 378).

- Of 31,910 non-empty apertures, **12,355 are not order-convex** (39%). The first failures are at |P| = 5.
- Neither factor is convex either. The not-world-dense family fails on Div12 itself (P = {0<2} ⊔ {1}, k = {0}: S = {2} and S = {0,1,2} are in it, S = {0,2} is not); the not-world-regular family first fails at four points.
- Of the non-empty apertures, 1,927 of 2,730 through six points are not up-sets and 2,730 − 173 are not principal up-sets. The Zₙ pattern is special.

**Smallest counterexample, inside the paper's own data [K].** Div180 = 2²·3²·5 has exponent poset P = {0<3} ⊔ {1<4} ⊔ {2}; the kernel 6 is the down-set {0,1}. Take S₁ = {0,3,4}, S₂ = S₁ ∪ {1}, S₃ = P. Then S₁ ⊆ S₂ ⊆ S₃, and j_{S₁} opens 6, j_{S₂} does not, j_{S₃} does (`aperture_not_convex_div180`). Concretely: under S₁ the bottom is ⊥ⱼ = {1,2}, j k = {0,1,2}, its world-negation is {1,2,4} ≠ ⊥ⱼ and its double negation {0,1,2,3} ≠ j k, so the four-fold is open. Adding the point 1 lowers the bottom to {2}; now j k = {0,1,2} is world-dense (its negation is {2} = ⊥ⱼ), and the four-fold closes. Restoring the full poset lowers the bottom again, to ∅, and k = {0,1} is ambiently ordinary.

**What holds instead.** Write M = min S for the minimal elements of S in the induced order.

> **Theorem (aperture criterion) [K]** (`opens_subNucleus_iff_minimal`). On D(P) for finite P, j_S opens k iff
> (i) some point of M lies outside k, and
> (ii) some s ∈ S outside k has every point of M below it inside k.

*Proof.* By the trace characterization (`opens_subNucleus_iff`, **[K]**), j_S opens k iff the trace D = k ∩ S is ordinary in D(S). In the down-set algebra of any finite poset Q, x ∈ Dᶜ iff nothing below x is in D, so x ∈ Dᶜᶜ iff every minimal point below x is in D (take a minimal point below any witness; `LowerSet.mem_compl_compl_iff_isMin`). Hence D is dense iff min Q ⊆ D (`LowerSet.compl_eq_bot_iff_isMin_mem`), and D is regular iff every point outside D has a minimal point below it outside D (`LowerSet.compl_compl_eq_self_iff_isMin`). Negate both with Q = S and D = k ∩ S. ∎

Three consequences, each verified across the whole sweep before being proved (`criterionFail: 0`, `bottomMismatch: 0`, `fiberNotUp: 0` in the script's output):

1. **The bottom is determined by M.** x ∈ j_S(⊥) iff no point of S lies below x (`mem_subNucleus_bot_iff`); so j_S(⊥) = P ∖ ↑S = P ∖ ↑M, and two observers have the same bottom iff they have the same minimal set (`minimal_mem_iff_of_subNucleus_bot_eq`). An observer that sees only its own atoms, S = M, opens nothing: condition (ii) has no candidate s. Its world is Boolean.
2. **Fiber monotonicity [K]** (`opens_subNucleus_of_subset_of_bot_eq`). If S ⊆ T and j_S(⊥) = j_T(⊥), then j_S opens k implies j_T opens k. Within each fiber of the bottom map, Ap(k) is an up-set, with minimal members M ∪ {w} for the w ∉ k, w ∉ M, with ↓w ∩ M ⊆ k. So **refinement that keeps the bottom never closes a four-fold; only refinement that lowers the bottom, revealing a point beneath something the coarser observer took as ground, can.** Every convexity failure in the sweep is of that form, and the Div180 witness shows the mechanism in one step (`bot_S₁_ne_bot_S₂`).
3. **The three-point core, proved.** On any Zₙ (n ≥ 6) with P the ladder of join-irreducibles q₁, q₂, q₃, …, kernel g = {q₁}: if q₁ ∉ S then M ∩ k = ∅ and (ii) has no candidate (every s ∈ S has some M-point below it), so q₁ ∈ S ⊆ M. In the ladder q₂ is the only point not above q₁, so (i) forces q₂ ∈ S; then M = {q₁, q₂}, and q₃ is the only point above q₁ but not above q₂, so (ii) forces q₃ ∈ S. Conversely any S ⊇ {q₁, q₂, q₃} satisfies both. Hence Ap(g) = {S : S ⊇ {q₁,q₂,q₃}} and |Ap(g)| = 2^{|P|−3}: the "one-eighth law" observed on Z₆–Z₁₄. This argument is stated here from the criterion and is **[computed]** at the level of the Zₙ instances (n = 6..14 in the sweep); it has not been formalized for general n. It passes unchanged to the full Rieger–Nishimura ladder as a statement about spatial nuclei.

**Latency is generic off products of chains [computed].** 18,685 of the 54,723 pairs have non-empty aperture without the kernel being ambiently ordinary. On divisor lattices latency was a corner phenomenon (Result 6.3); on general finite Heyting algebras it is the common case.

## 3. What this changes in the paper's framing

The aperture is not "robustness to blur": it is not monotone under refinement in either direction, and it is not even convex. The right picture is a fibration. The bottom map S ↦ min S (equivalently j ↦ j(⊥)) stratifies the nucleus lattice; the aperture is an up-set on each stratum; and it can switch off across strata when a refinement adds a point beneath an existing ground. The observer-reading in §8 of the paper should be stated this way: what an observer can and cannot see of a distinction's four-fold is governed first by *what it takes to be bottom*, and only then by how finely it resolves above that.

The convexity conjecture was the natural first guess and it is false at |P| = 5, inside an algebra the paper already tabulated. The structural statement that replaces it is a theorem, and a short one.
