# Preprints

Focused, peer-review-targeted mathematical papers extracted from the FalseWork framework. This directory holds artefacts in a different *register* from the broader framework essays in [`../papers/`](../papers/) — they are narrower in scope, mathematics-only in voice, and aimed at submission to mathematical venues (or pre-submission engagement on arXiv, Zulip, or specialist correspondence).

| `papers/` | `preprints/` |

### [`ordinary-elements-z6/`](ordinary-elements-z6/) — *The Unique Ordinary Element of a One-Generated Heyting Algebra, the Subgroup Lattice of ℤ/12ℤ, and a Characterization of n = p²q*

**Author.** Chris Brink (independent)
**Version.** Preprint, July 2026
**Status.** Not yet submitted; draft for specialist review. Candidate venues: math.LO arXiv posting; *Journal of Mathematics and Music* for the instance-facing exposition.
**One-line gloss.** Pure lattice theory extracted from the framework's mathematics anchor, stated with no framework vocabulary: the four-region decomposition of a Heyting algebra relative to an element is non-degenerate exactly at Citkin's *ordinary* elements (the non-degeneracy theorem); any Heyting algebra with an ordinary element has ≥ 6 elements and order-embeds Z₆ (threshold, generalizing Citkin Prop. 4(c)); a one-generated Heyting algebra has its generator as *unique* ordinary element — the statement is Citkin's (arXiv:2512.05633, unproved), the machine-checked proof apparently the first written, via a full formalization of Nishimura's one-variable normal form; the converse fails minimally at the 8-element `H₈ = Z₆ + Z₃`; the subgroup lattice of ℤ/12ℤ is one-generated by the tritone dyad and *is* Z₆; for every n = p₁^a₁⋯pₖ^aₖ the subgroup lattice has exactly ∏(aᵢ+1) − ∏aᵢ − 2^k + 1 ordinary elements, unique iff n = p²q with no restriction on the number of primes, least instance 12; and the substitution monoid End(Z₆) ≅ Z₆ has its identity pinned by the unique ordinary element, with every endomorphism satisfying f³ = f (a kernel-checked Ruitenburg instance).
**Formalization status.** All principal results kernel-checked in Lean 4 / Mathlib4 (`lean/FalseWorkPapers/Examples/`: `NishimuraKernelLaw.lean`, `NishimuraNormalForm.lean`, `LadderCore.lean`, `UniqueOrdinaryConverse.lean`, `WhyTwelve.lean`, `WhyTwelveGeneral.lean`, `SubstitutionMonoid.lean`, `GlivenkoCollapse.lean`); axiom audits standard axioms only, no `sorry`. One seam flagged in the paper: `H₈` minimality (script enumeration, not Lean).
**Prior-art standing.** Adjudicated by Citkin correspondence (June 2026, cited with permission); the paper states the standing result-by-result. Record at [`../docs/outreach/citkin-email.md`](../docs/outreach/citkin-email.md); claim files [`../validation/claims/ladder-core-threshold.md`](../validation/claims/ladder-core-threshold.md), [`../validation/claims/nishimura-normal-form.md`](../validation/claims/nishimura-normal-form.md), [`../validation/claims/why-twelve-tet.md`](../validation/claims/why-twelve-tet.md), [`../validation/claims/unique-ordinary-structure.md`](../validation/claims/unique-ordinary-structure.md).
**Companion documents.** The framework-register treatment of the same material is [`../papers/four-position-exposition.md`](../papers/four-position-exposition.md) §2.2–2.3a; the musical reading is confined to one flagged section (§9) of the preprint.

### [`four-position-partition/`](four-position-partition/) — *A Four-Position Partition of Morphisms in Elementary Topoi with Distinction Structure*

**Author.** Chris Brink (independent)
**Version.** Preprint, May 2026
**Status.** Not yet submitted; preprint draft for specialist engagement.
**One-line gloss.** Formalizes the framework's central structural claim as a theorem about elementary topoi: in any elementary topos with a non-trivial distinction structure, the morphism space partitions into exactly four pairwise-disjoint structural classes characterized by Heyting conditions on the image relative to the kernel image.
**Formalization status.** The theorem is kernel-checked in Lean 4 against Mathlib4; the supporting `HeytingAlgebra (Subobject Y)` instance for elementary topoi is upstreamed as [Mathlib PR #39618](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/39618).
**Companion documents.** The broader expository companion [`../papers/comma-formal-structure-note.md`](../papers/comma-formal-structure-note.md) states the same theorem in a framework-internal register alongside three other signature theorems and the named open *refusal-bridge* conjecture. The Lean source lives at [`../lean/FalseWorkPapers/Positions/`](../lean/FalseWorkPapers/) and is summarized in [`../lean/ARCHITECTURE.md`](../lean/ARCHITECTURE.md). A Spencer-Brown anchor — [`four-position-partition/spencer-brown-anchor.md`](four-position-partition/spencer-brown-anchor.md) — situates the theorem against its intellectual ancestor (*Laws of Form*, 1969) by showing that Definition 3.1 is the categorical lift of Spencer-Brown's calling axiom and reading each of the four cells as a Spencer-Brown register; Exploitation in particular is identified as the structural register that exists precisely because Spencer-Brown's crossing axiom fails in non-Boolean Heyting algebras. The anchor's two formalizable claims (Remark 5.3 partition-level Boolean collapse, Remark 5.5 idempotent-monad bridge) are mechanized in Lean at [`../lean/FalseWorkPapers/Positions/SpencerBrown.lean`](../lean/FalseWorkPapers/Positions/SpencerBrown.lean) and kernel-checked against only the standard Mathlib axioms. A canonization-closure-and-generator sketch — [`four-position-partition/closure-canonization.md`](four-position-partition/closure-canonization.md) — opens a third layer beyond classification and Commitment: the structural consequences of being Commitment-yes for the rest of the morphism space. A commitment-yes morphism is conjectured to (a) generate a reflective subcategory whose induced distinction structure produces a *recursive* secondary classification of every morphism in the topos (the *closure* layer), and (b) have a codomain that acts as a *separator* (generator) for the ambient category, making distinctions among later morphisms resolvable through composition with maps out of the canonization codomain (the *generator* refinement). Both conditional theorems — `recursive_partition` (closure) and `canonization_separation` (generator) — are mechanized in Lean at [`../lean/FalseWorkPapers/Positions/CanonizationClosure.lean`](../lean/FalseWorkPapers/Positions/CanonizationClosure.lean) and kernel-checked against the standard Mathlib axioms; the load-bearing question of *which* idempotent monads count as canonization closures of a given morphism is left open as substantive future work, while the separator condition is mathematically standard and concretely formalizable.

A first concrete kernel-checked `CanonizationGenerator` witness — the identity monad on `Discrete PUnit` — is at [`../lean/FalseWorkPapers/Examples/CanonizationGeneratorInstance.lean`](../lean/FalseWorkPapers/Examples/CanonizationGeneratorInstance.lean), establishing that the structure type is inhabited and that the conditional separation theorem fires on a concrete instance. The instantiation is structurally trivial (one object, one morphism) and is deliberately so — its purpose is type-level inhabitation, not framework-level non-vacuity. Richer examples (M-set, presheaf, domain-specific) are deferred as substantive future work per [`four-position-partition/closure-canonization.md`](four-position-partition/closure-canonization.md) §8a. A conditional cross-layer theorem `CanonizationGenerator.ofCommitmentYes` — recording that a Commitment-yes morphism with a separator codomain admits a canonization-generator witness — closes the architectural connection between the Commitment gate and the canonization layer at the type level; substantive content awaits the per-cell iteration content of `IsCommitmentYes` (cf. §8b).
**Active work-in-progress.** A *non-vacuity demonstration* (Tier 1 strengthening) is in development under [`four-position-partition/examples/`](four-position-partition/examples/). The goal is to exhibit a concrete elementary topos and distinction structure in which all four cells of the partition are simultaneously inhabited, and to mechanize the construction in Lean. Phase 1.1 is complete (absorbed into Remarks 5.4 and 5.5 of the paper). Phase 1.2 is *partially* complete: a hand-verified computation on a 4-element M-set in M-Set for an idempotent monoid M = {1, e, f} establishes a 6-element non-Boolean subobject lattice with a non-regular middle element, and shows that all four cells of the partition would be inhabited at that lattice-level configuration. The remaining task — constructing a distinction structure (D, η, ι) whose kernel image lands at that non-regular middle element — is blocked by a structural obstruction (standard reflections produce epi units, forcing the kernel image to be ⊤). Three forward paths are documented in [`four-position-partition/examples/phase-1-2-progress.md`](four-position-partition/examples/phase-1-2-progress.md).

## Submission and versioning conventions

- Markdown source is authoritative.
- When a preprint is submitted or accepted at a venue, the directory README is updated with the venue and submission state.
- Pre-submission revisions are tracked via git history; major revisions may be archived under `archive/`.
- AI-assisted drafting is disclosed in each paper per the project's [validation architecture](../docs/observations/validation-architecture-outcomes.md) and Paper 2's framework for epistemic dependency.
