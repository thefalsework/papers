## 1. The two questions

**(1) Converse conjecture.** The all-n law says: one-generated by an ordinary element ⟹ that element is the unique ordinary element. Conjecture: the converse — *a finite Heyting algebra with a unique ordinary element is one-generated (by that element)*. If true, uniqueness of the forced kernel would *characterize* logic's one-generated objects, strengthening the weld from "music's lattice happens to be `Z_6`" toward "any lattice with a forced kernel is a `Z_n`".

**(2) Glivenko collapse.** In a Boolean algebra `¬¬a = a` everywhere, so no element is ordinary and the four-position partition is degenerate for every choice of kernel. Since the regular elements of any Heyting algebra form a Boolean algebra (Glivenko; Mathlib `Heyting.Regular`), the double-negation reflection `a ↦ ¬¬a` lands every element in a subalgebra where the partition cannot be non-degenerate. Claim: the four positions are structure that the classical (Boolean) shadow of an intuitionistic algebra provably cannot carry.

## 2. Honest sequencing — what was found by hand before formalization

The converse conjecture was **refuted during the abstract proof attempt**, before any code ran. Recorded here as hand-mathematics (strength: unchecked prose) pending the kernel:

* **Candidate counterexample (10 elements).** Let `P` be the 4-element poset with minimal elements `p, q, n` and one element `r` above exactly `p` and `q`. Its downset lattice `H = D(P)` has 10 elements. Hand-computation: the unique ordinary element is `a = {p,q}` (with `¬a = {n}`, `¬¬a = {p,q,r}`); all nine other elements are regular or dense. But the subalgebra generated by `a` is `{⊥, a, ¬a, ¬¬a, a∨¬a, ⊤} ≅ Z_6` — ten minus four elements unreachable. Mechanism: the poset automorphism `p ↔ q` induces a Heyting automorphism fixing `a` and moving `{p}`; term values in `a` are fixed by every such automorphism, so `{p}` is not a term value.
* **Salvage lemma (general, hand-proved).** If `a` is the unique ordinary element of any Heyting algebra, then every dense element lies above `a ∨ ¬a` (so the dense elements are exactly the filter `↑(a∨¬a)`). Proof via the trichotomy: for dense `x`, `a ⊓ x` is neither regular nor dense, hence ordinary, hence `= a`; similarly `¬a ⊓ x = ¬a`.
* **A discarded intermediate.** An earlier hand-"theorem" (unique ordinary ⟹ regular skeleton is exactly `2²`) was **falsified during the same session** by the 10-element example itself, whose skeleton is `2³`. It is recorded here so it cannot resurface: uniqueness of the ordinary element does *not* bound the skeleton.

## 5. What the refutation means (interpretation, [A]-level, written at pre-registration)

If outcome (a) holds for check 1, then **a forced kernel does not imply one-generation**: "unique ordinary element" and "one-generated" are genuinely independent facts, and the weld needs both. Music's lattice satisfying *both* is more information than either alone — but conversely, no future retelling may compress "unique kernel" into "is a `Z_n`". The refutation also guards the program against its own pattern-hunger: the partition can be uniquely forced on algebras that are *not* logic's canonical objects.

The Glivenko collapse sharpens the trichotomy's "non-Boolean" clause into a reflection statement: it is not merely that Boolean algebras lack ordinary elements, but that the canonical classical shadow of *any* Heyting algebra destroys the partition. The four positions live strictly in the intuitionistic remainder.
