## 1. The two questions

**(1) Converse conjecture.** The all-n law says: one-generated by an ordinary element ⟹ that element is the unique ordinary element. Conjecture: the converse — *a finite Heyting algebra with a unique ordinary element is one-generated (by that element)*. If true, uniqueness of the forced kernel would *characterize* logic's one-generated objects, strengthening the weld from "music's lattice happens to be `Z_6`" toward "any lattice with a forced kernel is a `Z_n`".

**(2) Glivenko collapse.** In a Boolean algebra `¬¬a = a` everywhere, so no element is ordinary and the four-position partition is degenerate for every choice of kernel. Since the regular elements of any Heyting algebra form a Boolean algebra (Glivenko; Mathlib `Heyting.Regular`), the double-negation reflection `a ↦ ¬¬a` lands every element in a subalgebra where the partition cannot be non-degenerate. Claim: the four positions are structure that the classical (Boolean) shadow of an intuitionistic algebra provably cannot carry.

## 2. Honest sequencing — what was found by hand before formalization

The converse conjecture was **refuted during the abstract proof attempt**, before any code ran. Recorded here as hand-mathematics (strength: unchecked prose) pending the kernel:

* **Candidate counterexample (10 elements).** Let `P` be the 4-element poset with minimal elements `p, q, n` and one element `r` above exactly `p` and `q`. Its downset lattice `H = D(P)` has 10 elements. Hand-computation: the unique ordinary element is `a = {p,q}` (with `¬a = {n}`, `¬¬a = {p,q,r}`); all nine other elements are regular or dense. But the subalgebra generated by `a` is `{⊥, a, ¬a, ¬¬a, a∨¬a, ⊤} ≅ Z_6` — ten minus four elements unreachable. Mechanism: the poset automorphism `p ↔ q` induces a Heyting automorphism fixing `a` and moving `{p}`; term values in `a` are fixed by every such automorphism, so `{p}` is not a term value.
* **Salvage lemma (general, hand-proved).** If `a` is the unique ordinary element of any Heyting algebra, then every dense element lies above `a ∨ ¬a` (so the dense elements are exactly the filter `↑(a∨¬a)`). Proof via the trichotomy: for dense `x`, `a ⊓ x` is neither regular nor dense, hence ordinary, hence `= a`; similarly `¬a ⊓ x = ¬a`.
* **A discarded intermediate.** An earlier hand-"theorem" (unique ordinary ⟹ regular skeleton is exactly `2²`) was **falsified during the same session** by the 10-element example itself, whose skeleton is `2³`. It is recorded here so it cannot resurface: uniqueness of the ordinary element does *not* bound the skeleton.

## 3. Pre-registered checks and outcomes (written before running)

1. **Lean verification of the counterexample** (`Examples/UniqueOrdinaryConverse.lean`): build `H` as the subtype of downward-closed `Finset (Fin 4)`; `decide` the Heyting laws, `decide` unique-ordinariness of `a`; prove non-generation via a general lemma (`HeytingHom`s commute with `nishimuraTerm`) plus the swap automorphism. **Outcome (a):** all checks pass — conjecture refuted [K]. **Outcome (b):** the hand-analysis erred and `decide` rejects some step — conjecture reverts to open and the error is reported here.
2. **Exhaustive enumeration for minimality** (`scripts/unique-ordinary-search.py`): generate *all* finite distributive lattices of cardinality ≤ 12 (as downset lattices `D(P)`, incremental poset generation with downset-count pruning — complete for this size by Birkhoff duality); for each with a unique ordinary element, check one-generation by closure. **Outcome (a):** 10 is the minimal counterexample cardinality. **Outcome (b):** a smaller counterexample exists — it becomes the headline and the 10-element example is kept as the symmetric specimen. **Outcome (c):** the enumeration contradicts the hand example — investigate before anything ships.
3. **Glivenko collapse in Lean** (`Examples/GlivenkoCollapse.lean`): `boolean_no_ordinary` (no Boolean algebra has an ordinary element), `boolean_no_kernel` (via the trichotomy: no four-cell kernel either), `compl_compl_not_ordinary` (the image of the double-negation reflection is never ordinary), and the instantiation on `Heyting.Regular H`. These are short abstract proofs; failure mode is Mathlib API mismatch only.

## 4. Results (filled in after the checks — see status line)

**Check 2 fired outcome (b): the enumeration found a counterexample smaller than the hand example.** The complete search over all finite distributive lattices of cardinality ≤ 12 (11,592 poset nodes explored, 237 lattices with a unique ordinary element) reports counterexamples at sizes **8**, 9, 10, 11, 12 — the minimal one has **8 elements**, not 10. The 10-element hand example of §2 is correct but not minimal; the headline moved to the 8-element witness, as the pre-registration prescribed.

* **`H8` [K]** (`Examples/UniqueOrdinaryConverse.lean`): the downset lattice of the 5-element poset `0 < 1 < 3 < 4`, `2 < 3`. Kernel-checked, all by `decide` over the 8 elements:
  * `H8.ordinary_unique` — `a` (downset `{0}`) is the unique ordinary element;
  * `H8.kernel_unique` — equivalently, the unique non-degenerate four-cell kernel;
  * `H8.not_nishimura_generated` — **`H8` is not Nishimura-generated by `a`**: the seven elements `≠ d` form a closed predicate containing `a` (checked by `decide`), and the general lemma `nishimuraTerm_mem_of_closed` [K] confines every term value to it. The dense join-irreducible `d` (downset `{0,1,2,3}`) is unreachable — the term ladder in `a` stabilizes after seven values and skips it.
  * Bundle: `unique_ordinary_converse_false` (axioms: `propext, Quot.sound`).
* **The converse of the all-n law is FALSE [K].** With Nishimura's theorem [C] (one-generated ⟹ term-enumerated) and `nishimura_ordinary_unique` (an ordinary generator of `H8` would have to be `a`), `H8` is not one-generated at all. Unlike the §2 hand example, the minimal witness needs no automorphism argument: the obstruction is early stabilization of the term ladder, not symmetry.
* **The instructive pair at cardinality 8**: `Z_8` (one-generated, unique ordinary element) and `H8` (not one-generated, unique ordinary element) — uniqueness of the kernel cannot distinguish them.
* **Salvage lemma [K], general** (`unique_ordinary_dense_iff`, any Heyting algebra): if `a` is the unique ordinary element, then `x` is dense iff `a ⊔ ¬a ≤ x` — the dense part is the principal filter over the generator's excluded middle. Checked concretely on `H8` (`H8.dense_filter`).
* **Glivenko collapse [K]** (`Examples/GlivenkoCollapse.lean`): `boolean_no_ordinary` / `boolean_no_kernel` (no Boolean algebra carries an ordinary element or a non-degenerate kernel); `not_isOrdinary_compl_compl` (in any Heyting algebra, `¬¬a` is never ordinary — the reflection annihilates ordinariness pointwise; axioms: `propext` only); `glivenko_no_kernel` / `glivenko_collapse` (the Boolean algebra `Heyting.Regular H` of any Heyting algebra carries no four-cell kernel). The four positions do not survive passage to the classical shadow.
* **Minimality is [C]**, resting on the enumeration script, not on Lean. The counterexample itself is [K]. (The Lean proofs use plain `decide` throughout — no `native_decide` anywhere in the project; a `native_decide` would surface as `Lean.ofReduceBool` in the axiom audit, and the audit reports `propext, Quot.sound` only.)

## 4a. Completeness cross-check of the enumeration (2026-06-10, post-publication verification)

The minimality claim's trust chain was audited against the one place it could silently break: *did the enumeration really range over all finite Heyting algebras of cardinality ≤ 12, or a narrower class?* Two answers, one mathematical and one empirical:

* **Mathematical**: finite Heyting algebras = finite distributive lattices [C, standard]; by Birkhoff duality these are in bijection (up to iso) with finite posets; the script generates every poset along its linear extensions and prunes only on the monotone invariant `|D(P)|`. Complete by construction — *if the code is right*.
* **Empirical** (`scripts/verify-enumeration-oeis.py`): an independent re-run that canonicalizes each generated poset up to isomorphism and counts distinct distributive lattices per cardinality. Result: **exact agreement with OEIS A006982** (Erné–Heitzig–Reinhold 2002, *On the Number of Distributive Lattices*) at every size — `1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 15, 26, 47, 82, 151` for `n = 1..12`, 342 lattices total, no gaps, no surplus. The enumeration provably visited every finite Heyting algebra in scope at least once.
* **Sharpening from the iso-class census**: `H8` is the **unique** counterexample at cardinality 8 up to isomorphism (the landscape: 1 class at size 8, 2 at 9, 6 at 10, 9 at 11, 19 at 12 — counterexamples proliferate above the threshold, exactly as kernels did above `p²q` in the why-12 sweep).
* The cross-check is still [C] (Python against a published census, not Lean); what it upgrades is the *confidence* in the [C], not its tag. Minimality stays [C] and must not circulate as [K].

## 5. What the refutation means (interpretation, [A]-level, written at pre-registration)

If outcome (a) holds for check 1, then **a forced kernel does not imply one-generation**: "unique ordinary element" and "one-generated" are genuinely independent facts, and the weld needs both. Music's lattice satisfying *both* is more information than either alone — but conversely, no future retelling may compress "unique kernel" into "is a `Z_n`". The refutation also guards the program against its own pattern-hunger: the partition can be uniquely forced on algebras that are *not* logic's canonical objects.

The Glivenko collapse sharpens the trichotomy's "non-Boolean" clause into a reflection statement: it is not merely that Boolean algebras lack ordinary elements, but that the canonical classical shadow of *any* Heyting algebra destroys the partition. The four positions live strictly in the intuitionistic remainder.
